Dental implants abroad: what to check before you book

An implant abroad can save you thousands – if the clinic is right. These 10 checks help you decide well, wherever you are treated.

Is it worth getting implants abroad?

In the UK a private implant with crown usually costs about £2,000–3,000. In countries like Kosovo it is a fraction of that – at Medident in Pejë from €600. The difference comes from rent, wages and lab fees, not necessarily from lower-quality materials.

What matters most is not the price but the quality of planning, the implant itself and the aftercare.

10 checks before you decide

  1. Who places the implant? Ask for the name, speciality and experience (years, number of implants).
  2. Which implant brand? Good clinics name the brand (e.g. MegaGen, Straumann, Nobel) and give you an implant passport.
  3. 3D scan (CBCT)? Without one, bone, nerve and sinus cannot be assessed safely.
  4. Written plan with a fixed price. You should know what will be done and what it costs before you fly.
  5. Realistic timeline. Implants usually need 3–4 months to heal – “everything in 5 days” is not right for everyone.
  6. Honest talk about bone grafts. Whether you need one is shown by the CBCT, not a package deal.
  7. Sterilisation and hygiene. Ask how instruments are sterilised and what is single-use.
  8. Aftercare. How do you reach the clinic once you are home? Are the terms in writing?
  9. Communication. Do you understand your dentist? Is there support in your language?
  10. Real reviews. Google reviews and before/after photos of real patients.

Frequently asked questions

Are dental implants abroad dangerous?

Not in themselves. The risk depends on experience, planning (CBCT), implant quality, hygiene and aftercare – exactly what you should check first.

How much can I save with implants abroad?

Compared with the UK, often well over £1,500 per implant with crown, even after flights and accommodation.
